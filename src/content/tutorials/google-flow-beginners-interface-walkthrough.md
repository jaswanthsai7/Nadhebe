> **Direct Answer for Beginners:** **Google Flow Storyboard Studio** is structured into four sequential tabs: **Step 1 (Script Import)**, **Step 2 (Character Setup)**, **Step 3 (Shot Breakdown)**, and **Step 4 (Video Render & Export)**. To generate your first AI video clip in under 10 minutes, paste a 3-sentence narrative into Step 1, accept the default character reference in Step 2, select an establishing shot framing in Step 3, and click **Generate Video** to synthesize a 4-second clip powered by Google Veo.

Opening a new creative tool can feel overwhelming when greeted by blank canvas boards, technical prompt fields, and unfamiliar cinematography dropdowns. Google Flow was designed specifically to bridge the gap between creative writing and visual filmmaking, allowing anyone to turn words into motion pictures.

This complete beginner's walkthrough breaks down every panel, toolbar, and settings drawer, guiding you from your first blank project to a rendered, downloadable video.

---

## 1. Navigating the Workspace: The Four Foundational Zones

When you open Google Flow, the interface is split into four primary operational zones:

[SCREENSHOT: Labeled diagram of the Google Flow interface pointing out Header Bar, Stage Timeline, Inspector Drawer, and Asset Bin]

1. **The Navigation Header (Top):** Houses the Project Title, Version History, Daily Credit Meter, and the 4-Step Progress Bar (`Script` $\rightarrow$ `Characters` $\rightarrow$ `Shots` $\rightarrow$ `Render`).
2. **The Storyboard Canvas (Center):** Your visual stage. This is where your script's scenes appear as modular, re-orderable panel cards.
3. **The Shot Inspector Drawer (Right):** Opens when you click any panel card. Allows you to adjust prompt wording, camera framing, lens focal length, and lighting mood.
4. **The Media & Asset Bin (Bottom Left):** Stores generated character turnaround portraits, audio voiceover tracks, and rendered Veo MP4 video clips.

---

## 2. Step 1: Script Ingestion and Scene Breakdown

Everything in Google Flow begins with a script. You do not need an elaborate 90-page Hollywood screenplay; even three sentences of descriptive prose will suffice.

[SCREENSHOT: Step 1 Script Import panel showing text pasted in and the auto-detected scene cards highlighted]

### Quick-Start Action:
1. Click **New Project** and select **Start with Script**.
2. Type or paste your scene into the text box:
   ```text
   EXT. OLD LIGHTHOUSE - DUSK
   A storm brews over the North Atlantic. ELI (60s, weathered yellow oilskin jacket) climbs the spiral cast-iron stairs of the lighthouse tower. He wipes rain from his glass lantern.
   ```
3. Click **Analyze & Break Down**. Google Flow's parser instantly categorizes the location, weather, lighting, and characters.

---

## 3. Step 2: Binding Characters and Style References

Before generating shots, Google Flow prompts you to define how your characters and visual world look.

[SCREENSHOT: Step 2 Character binding drawer showing the reference upload slot and color palette selector]

- **Character Portraits:** Upload an existing photo or click **Generate Concept** to let the model generate a character face based on your script description.
- **Global Visual Style:** Choose your project's aesthetic from presets including **Cinematic 35mm**, **Graphic Novel**, **Anime**, or **Vintage Documentary**.
- **Aspect Ratio:** Toggle between **16:9 Widescreen** (YouTube, Film) or **9:16 Vertical** (Shorts, Reels, TikTok).
- To ensure your protagonist doesn't morph between scenes, follow our rules in [how to keep characters consistent in Google Flow](https://nadhebe.com/tutorials/how-to-keep-characters-consistent-in-google-flow/).

---

## 4. Step 3: Directing the Camera in the Shot Breakdown Grid

Step 3 is where the magic happens. Your scene is presented as a chronological row of visual panels.

[SCREENSHOT: Step 3 panel card displaying camera framing dropdown, seed lock icon, and re-generate button]

### Key Controls on Every Panel Card
- **Shot Type Dropdown:** Choose your camera distance (**Extreme Wide**, **Medium Shot**, **Close-Up**, **Dutch Angle**).
- **Prompt Refinement Box:** Add specific micro-actions (e.g., *"Eli turns the brass valve"*).
- **Seed Lock Button (Padlock Icon):** Locks the composition so future prompt edits only change small details.
- **Generate Frame (1 Credit):** Synthesizes a static 2D image preview in 5 seconds to verify blocking and lighting.

---

## 5. Step 4: Rendering and Assembling Your First Video

Once your static storyboard panel looks right, it's time to bring it to life using **Google Veo**.

[SCREENSHOT: Step 4 video timeline showing the playback head, play button, and export resolution options]

1. Click **Generate Video** on Panel 01.
2. In the modal, select **Motion Intensity: 4 (Balanced)**.
3. Wait 30–45 seconds. Veo synthesizes a full 4.0-second 1080p video clip with realistic physics, lighting, and camera motion.
4. Click the **Play** button on the panel to review your clip in real-time.
5. Click **Export Scene** in the upper right to download your video as a high-bitrate MP4 file. If you ever run into render errors or missing buttons, consult our troubleshooting guide on [fixing Google Flow Storyboard Studio errors](https://nadhebe.com/tutorials/google-flow-storyboard-studio-not-working/).
